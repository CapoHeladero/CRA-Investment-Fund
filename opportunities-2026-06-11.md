## Market context

Markets turned risk-off into 11 June. The S&P 500 closed **7,266.99 on 10 June (−1.62%)**, the Dow shed ~953 points (−1.87%) and the Nasdaq fell −1.98%. The index sits roughly **+5.9% above its rising 200-day moving average (~6,863)** — still in an uptrend, but the cushion is thinning. Two drivers did the damage: a **hotter-than-expected May CPI (+4.2% YoY, a three-year high)** that revived inflation worries, and **renewed Iran tension** after President Trump said negotiations were taking "too long" and threatened further action — pushing **WTI to ~$90 and Brent to ~$93**. Layered on top, **semiconductors have led the slide** since Broadcom's softer-than-hoped AI guidance (the Nasdaq's worst session since April 2025 came on 4 June, when ~$1T was wiped from chip names).

Rates and policy are steady: the **10Y is 4.52%, the 2Y 4.15%** (curve positive, not inverted), and the Fed is expected to **hold the 3.50–3.75% range** at the 16–17 June FOMC. The **VIX has firmed back toward ~21**. Leadership remains where it has been all year — **Energy (+~22% YTD), Materials, Industrials and Consumer Staples** — while mega-cap tech and semis lag. On the nine-point bear checklist, only two flags are up: **active geopolitical risk** and **signs of mega-cap/tech distribution**. Net read: a maturing, rotation-driven bull with rising headline risk — favour cash-generative real-economy leaders, keep speculative themes small, and let the higher-beta names come to your levels.
